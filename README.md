
# A/B Test Analysis: Ad Campaign Performance

## Project Overview

This project evaluates an advertising campaign using an A/B test. It compares users exposed to advertisements (the **ad group**) with users shown public service announcements (the **PSA control group**) to measure advertising effectiveness.

The analysis focuses on conversion rates, advertising reach, ad frequency, incremental revenue, and campaign profitability.

## Key Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Users in Ad Group | 564,577 |
| Users in PSA Control Group | 23,524 |
| Ad Group Conversion Rate | 2.56% |
| PSA Group Conversion Rate | 1.79% |
| Absolute Conversion Lift | 0.77 percentage points |
| Relative Conversion Lift | 43.1% |
| Additional Conversions | 4,343 |
| Ad Impressions | 14.0 million |
| PSA Impressions | 582,000 |
| Revenue from Incremental Conversions | $86,860 |
| Advertising Spend | $140,147 |
| Net Profit/Loss | -$53,287 |
| Revenue per Incremental Conversion | $20.00 |
| Break-Even Value per Conversion | $32.27 |

## Key Findings

### 1. Conversion Performance

The ad group achieved a conversion rate of **2.56%**, compared with **1.79%** for the PSA control group.

This represents:
- An absolute lift of **0.77 percentage points**
- A relative conversion lift of **43.1%**
- Approximately **4,343 additional conversions** attributed to the campaign

The results indicate that the campaign improved conversion performance compared with the control group.

### 2. Financial Impact

Despite the improvement in conversions, the campaign was not profitable.

| Financial Metric | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Revenue from Incremental Conversions | $86,860 |
| Advertising Cost | $140,147 |
| Net Loss | -$53,287 |

The current revenue value per incremental conversion is **$20**, while the reported break-even value is **$32.27**.

Therefore, the additional revenue generated by the campaign did not cover its advertising costs.

### 3. Effect of Ad Frequency

The dashboard shows higher conversion rates among users exposed to more advertisements.

| Ad Exposure | Ad Group Conversion Rate | PSA Group Conversion Rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| 51–100 ads | 11.6% | 5.8% |
| 101–500 impressions | 17.1% | 11.9% |

These findings suggest an association between higher ad exposure and higher observed conversion rates. However, the results alone do not establish that increasing ad frequency directly causes higher conversions.

### 4. Test Group Distribution

The experiment included:
- **564,577 users** in the ad group
- **23,524 users** in the PSA control group

The ad group was approximately 24 times larger than the control group.

## Business Recommendations

The campaign improved conversions but failed to generate a positive financial return.

To improve profitability, the dashboard identifies two potential targets:

1. **Increase conversion value:** Raise the value per incremental conversion from $20 to approximately $32.27, an increase of about 61%.
2. **Reduce advertising costs:** Decrease advertising spend by approximately 38%.

Ad frequency should also be evaluated carefully to determine whether additional exposure generates enough incremental revenue to justify its cost.

## Dashboard Highlights

The dashboard provides insights into:

- A/B test conversion performance
- Absolute and relative conversion lift
- Advertising and PSA impression volumes
- Conversion rates by ad exposure frequency
- Incremental revenue and advertising costs
- Campaign profit and loss
- Break-even conversion value

## Conclusion

The advertising campaign generated a **43.1% relative lift in conversions**, demonstrating improved conversion performance compared with the PSA control group.

However, the campaign incurred a **net loss of $53,287** because the revenue generated by incremental conversions was lower than the advertising spend.

The main business takeaway is that **higher conversion rates do not necessarily translate into profitability**. Future campaign decisions should consider both conversion effectiveness and the financial value of each incremental conversion.
